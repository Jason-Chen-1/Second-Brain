# D. Ranked Desiderata

## D.1 Ranked List

| Rank | Desideratum | Why It Matters for Lab Research | Status | Project Evidence | Success Test and Threshold | Tradeoff |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Source fidelity.** Facts in pages and answers must be entailed by the designated sources; speculation and cross-source inferences must be labeled as `synthesis` or `source-needed`. | This prevents the model from filling gaps with parametric knowledge and leading researchers to believe that a claim came from the registered source set. | Partial | `AGENTS.md` requires every nontrivial factual claim to include a source ID such as `[Sxx]`, but the project does not yet have a systematic entailment audit. | Audit 30 claim-source pairs: at least 95% must be entailed and none may be contradicted; 100% of source-external inferences must be labeled. | Strict fidelity may reduce fluent supplementation and ambitious synthesis; explicitly labeled synthesis should remain permissible. |
| 2 | **Citation correctness.** Every `[Sxx]` citation must support the subject, time frame, numerical details, causal strength, and scope of the adjacent claim. | Incorrect citations create an illusion of verifiability, and researchers may become less cautious simply because a source ID is present. | Partial | Ingest evidence includes item-level checks. | Randomly audit 20 claim-citation pairs: at least 95% must be fully entailed; claims involving numbers, dates, versions, or normative terms must achieve 100% correctness; the number of contradicted pairs must be zero. | Fine-grained citation practices increase verification costs and page density. |
| 3 | **Coverage of critical information.** Ingest must preserve mechanisms, major findings, numerical results, limitations, negative findings, sample conditions, uncertainty, and scope of applicability rather than recording conclusions alone. | A factually correct but incomplete summary can cause subsequent research designs to rely on omitted information. | Partial | The ingest prompt explicitly requires the extraction of limitations, negative results, and uncertainty. | Build a gold claim set for five sources; recall must be at least 90% separately for core claims, limitations, and negative evidence, while major limitations must achieve 100% coverage. | Greater coverage increases page length and noise, so information must be presented in layers. |
| 4 | **Appropriate abstention and avoidance of overconfidence.** When evidence is insufficient, a question is ambiguous, its premise is false, or its version is unspecified, the system must decline to give a definitive answer or ask for clarification. | Confident guesses can enter persistent memory and contaminate future queries and research judgments. | Partial | The schema requires `source-needed` when no supporting source is available; Q04 and Q08 report uncertainty and evidence gaps. | Construct 20 queries, with 10 answerable and 10 unanswerable. Recall for abstaining on unanswerable questions must be at least 90%, accuracy on answerable questions must be at least 90%, and the number of unsupported definitive claims must be zero. | Excessive abstention reduces usefulness; the system must distinguish among refusal, a qualified answer, and a request for clarification. |
| 5 | **Research actionability.** Pages and answers should help researchers identify the next experiment, reading task, comparison, or decision, while clearly stating the object and intended purpose of the action rather than merely restating information. | Users need a memory system that advances research. Even correct content may amount to little more than a higher-maintenance repository if it does not help determine the next step. | Partial | Q06-Q08 produced structured comparisons and conditional judgments, and Q07 organized a reliability stack. | Use 10 real research questions. For at least 80% of the answers, two researchers must independently arrive at the same concrete next step, including an action, object, and expected outcome. A generic recommendation to “continue researching” does not count as a pass. | Emphasizing action may prematurely compress exploratory knowledge into recommendations; evidence, interpretation, and next action should appear in separate fields so that recommendations are not presented as facts. |
| 6 | **Preservation of constraints and schema.** After repeated ingest, query, and maintenance operations, the system must retain hard constraints governing frontmatter, source IDs, wikilinks, naming, logs, and the read-only status of raw sources. | Content may be correct in an individual operation, but gradual rule loss will make the expanding knowledge base structurally unreliable and difficult to inspect. | Partial | Current link and frontmatter checks pass, but the evaluator has drifted out of sync with the verdict format. | Decompose the schema into at least 25 atomic constraints. After repeated modifications, the hard-constraint pass rate must be at least 98%, the strict operation pass rate at least 90%, and the counts of raw-source modifications, unknown sources, and broken links must all remain zero. | Additional hard constraints reduce flexibility and increase maintenance costs; hard constraints must be distinguished from judgment-based guidelines. |
| 7 | **Explainability of Create-vs-Update decisions.** The system must explain why it created a new page, updated an existing page, or wrote nothing, and it must list the candidate pages and decision criteria considered. | An incorrect create decision causes fragmentation, an incorrect update decision conflates concepts, and an incorrect no-write decision discards reusable knowledge. | Partial | `AGENTS.md` defines the relevant rules, ingest logs record created and modified files, and T03 shows that adjacent concepts can be kept distinct; however, the project does not systematically evaluate decision accuracy. | Create human gold decisions for 10 cases. Agreement on create, update, or no-write decisions must be at least 90%; 100% of decisions must list candidate pages and rule-based reasons, and the orphan rate for new pages must be zero. | Explanations consume tokens and logging capacity and may become post-hoc rationalizations; they should cite observable rules rather than preserve unverified, long-form chains of thought. |
| 8 | **Appropriate knowledge granularity.** Each page or knowledge unit should center on one topic: adjacent but independent concepts must not be merged into one page, while a complete concept must not be split into fragments that cannot be understood independently. | Granularity directly affects subsequent updating, comparison, and reuse. Units that are too coarse couple unrelated material to the same update; units that are too fine create duplication, orphan pages, and excessive navigation. | Partial | T03 keeps MCP architecture and authorization on two interlinked pages, while S22 uses a separate disambiguation page to prevent conceptual confusion; however, the project does not systematically measure mixed-topic pages, fragmentation, or duplication. | Have two reviewers inspect 20 pages for a single stable topic. Both the mixed-topic page rate and the unnecessary-fragment rate must be at most 10%, with inter-rater agreement of at least 0.80. Every resulting page must be independently definable and have a genuine reuse scenario. | Finer granularity improves reuse but increases the number of pages and the navigation burden; coarser granularity reduces navigation but increases overload and update coupling. |
| 9 | **Knowledge navigability.** Through the index, topic pages, and wikilinks, users should be able to understand what the knowledge base covers and locate the concepts, comparisons, and limitations they need without knowing exact filenames. | The value of a Second Brain lies not only in preserving content but also in creating an intelligible knowledge space; knowledge that cannot be found is functionally equivalent to missing knowledge. | Partial | `wiki/index.md` organizes pages by topic. | Ask an RA who did not participate in the build to complete 10 tasks, including locating a concept, comparing adjacent methods, finding limitations, and returning to the relevant topic. The success rate must be at least 90%, median completion time no more than three minutes, and the incorrect-navigation rate no more than 10%. | Additional links and categories can improve discoverability, but too many entry points create visual noise and classification overhead; success should be measured through task completion rather than link count. |
| 10 | **Usability under scale growth.** As the number of sources, pages, and links increases, the speed of finding information, difficulty of comprehension, and quality of task completion should not deteriorate rapidly. | Usability with slightly more than 20 sources does not imply usability after a lab accumulates 100 or 500 sources; a genuine research memory must remain useful as it grows. | Partial | With more than 20 sources, no obvious errors appeared in the index, links, context handling, or consistency. The current project contains 24 sources and 20 wiki pages, but this judgment lacks staged task data, quantified latency, and larger-scale testing. | Repeat the same 10 navigation and research tasks on snapshots containing 20, 50, and 100 sources. The task success rate may decline by no more than 10 percentage points, median completion time may increase by no more than 50%, and broken-link and orphan rates must remain zero; manual maintenance time must also be recorded. | Maintaining usability at scale may require stronger indexing, hierarchical organization, and additional maintenance effort. |

## D.2 Why the Current Top Five Matter Most

### 1. Source Fidelity

Source fidelity determines whether the system is compiling research materials or using the language model's general knowledge to complete a merely plausible story.

### 2. Citation Correctness

Source fidelity must be implemented through specific citations. Incorrect citations are particularly dangerous because they can lead users to believe that a claim has already been verified.

### 3. Coverage of Critical Information

A research memory cannot preserve paper conclusions alone. Limitations, negative findings, sample conditions, and scope of applicability often determine whether a result can transfer to a new experiment; omitting them can make a page misleading even when it contains no direct factual errors.

### 4. Appropriate Abstention and Avoidance of Overconfidence

Research questions frequently extend beyond the current corpus. A reliable system must recognize the boundaries of its evidence and state what is missing instead of using parametric knowledge to generate a definitive answer.

### 5. Research Actionability

Ultimately, a lab must use its knowledge to decide what to do next rather than merely confirm what the system has stored.
